The Mathematical Inscription
of the World (installation)

(2026) installation / BA grad work

CNC and manually-machined 6061 aluminum, various mold components, T-slot aluminum extrusion structures, fasteners, nitrile hoses, ferric (III) chloride, HDPE canister, peristaltic pump, servo motor, arduino nano, buck converter, power supply



Through two interlinked parts, an installation and a short film (view film page), The Mathematical Inscription of the World works with the semantic dimensionality of molds. The conceptual notion of a mold is applied in the context of contemporary knowledge systems and image-driven reality production in today's society.

Modern technology is fundamentally a product of Western history. With its spread across the globe, it carried its European ways of thinking, driven by objectivity, computability and scientific knowledge. This hegemonic force however, has necessitated all other philosophies be translated and trialed against its Western framework, a homogenizing process of erasure. A framework that proclaims it has transcended its status as a system of metaphors and become “what the world is.”

The installation is formed around an industrial injection mold, a tool used to produce most consumer objects. Lacking an imprint of an object to mold, it takes on a symbolic role as an allegory of said modern technology. The cooling oil recirculating in its internal channel system has been replaced with a metal etchant, which slowly leaks out, scarring its precision-machined surface. The mold attempts to conceal this internal, non-conformal flow, intensifying its selective compatibility.



The injection mold is made up of 88 individual components and 10 machined parts, four of which were manually machined, while the rest was CNC-machined. The design is entirely my own and is manufactured to +/- 0.1mm precision. Total weight of the main assembly exceeds 45kg.
